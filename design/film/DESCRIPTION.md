## Lighting, realism and site-intro value

The reference uses warm orange plating against cyan/teal water, punctuated by yellow hardware and narrow work lights. Suspended haze, localized visibility, chipped coating, fouling, seams, robot contact and changing orientations give scale and friction to the synthetic environment. Much of the credibility also comes from the workflow: explicit conditions, failure criteria, diagnosis, revisions, validation and unresolved exceptions. Those details make the story feel accountable, although the stills alone cannot verify the claims.

The hero is readable without dialogue: a small machine, difficult work, a visible consequence. Its spare lower area can support surrounding site layout, and a short visual incident can reward attention immediately. The longer film then explains why the incident matters. The hero's exact repeat point is not observable; a hard reset from falling to attached could visibly jump.

For our adaptation, preserve vulnerability followed by rehearsal and earned calm. Change the visual grammar: daylight and air, vacuum feet instead of wheels, a high-rise viewed through glass and down its vertical face, tactile seal detail, and spatial test worlds instead of terminals, tiled dashboards, code or before/after grids. Do not reproduce the hero's orange ceiling above dark water or its centered crawler composition.
